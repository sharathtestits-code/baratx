# BaratX — Gen Z Go-To-Market Plan
Prepared: August 9, 2026

## Grounding

This plan is built on three things I actually have evidence for, not assumptions: (1) the live product as it exists today — live sided-debate rooms, six Arenas, a Startups-first density push, a Founding ₹150 creator incentive, existing WhatsApp and X channels; (2) sourced 2026 Gen Z research on content formats, trust, and platform behavior (Sprout Social, SocialChamp, Meltwater India, Netinfluencer — cited in the prior audit); (3) the specific tension that research surfaced: your current "No Reels required" positioning bets against the single most consistent Gen Z data point (short-form video dominance), while your live-room mechanic and niche-community structure are genuine data-backed fits. This GTM plan is built to resolve that tension deliberately, not ignore it.

---

## 1. The GTM thesis, one line

**Market the product with the short-form video Gen Z actually watches. Build the product around the live, real, un-algorithmed debate Gen Z says they're tired of not having.**

You don't need to add a Reels tab to BaratX to win Gen Z attention — you need short, clippable, shareable video *about* what happens inside BaratX (a live room going off, a savage "Fund it or Pass" verdict, a daily question hitting a nerve) to pull people in. The product stays text-and-live-audio; the acquisition layer speaks fluent short-form video. That's not a contradiction — it's how most successful "third space" communities (Discord servers, Reddit communities, Clubhouse in its early days) actually grew: video and memes at the top of the funnel, a genuinely different experience once you're inside.

---

## 2. Who exactly — the ICP

**Primary: Indian Gen Z, 18–24, college students and early-career (0–3 years into a job), Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities, English-plus-Hindi comfortable, active on Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Telegram, and WhatsApp groups, opinionated about campus life, cricket, Bollywood/regional cinema, and "hustle"/side-hustle content.**

This is narrower than "India's public square" and that's intentional — you cannot GTM to everyone at once with a near-zero user base. Two usable entry wedges within this ICP, run in parallel at different intensity:

- **Wedge A — Campus & Culture (broad, viral, top-of-funnel):** Entertainment, Sports, and the daily Square question. This is where clip-based, meme-native content travels fastest and where a first-time Gen Z visitor finds something to react to in five seconds.
- **Wedge B — Builders & Hustle (narrower, monetizable, matches your existing Startups density):** reframe the Startups arena's "Fund it or Pass" not as VC jargon but as hustle-culture content — the same genre as the side-hustle, founder-story, "0 to 1" content Gen Z already follows on Instagram. This keeps your existing product investment (48 trending tags, Founding incentive, richest Arena) relevant to Gen Z rather than abandoning it.

Run Wedge A for reach and proof-of-life; run Wedge B for the audience most likely to actually pay for something eventually. Don't split budget evenly — Wedge A should get roughly 70% of GTM effort in the first 60 days, because it's what gets a debate room from "0 takes" to visibly alive, which is the precondition for everything else.

---

## 3. Product gate — what must be true before you spend a rupee on acquisition

GTM spend into an empty room is the fastest way to burn a Gen Z visitor's trust permanently — this audience does not give second chances to something that looked dead on first visit. Three gates, in order:

1. **At least one Arena (recommend Entertainment or Sports, not Startups, for the Gen Z push) has real, visible, non-official-account replies on its top 3–5 live debates before any paid or organic push goes out.** Seed this yourself with 10–15 people you know personally if you have to — a debate with 8 real replies beats a feed of 50 auto-posted headlines with zero replies, every time.
2. **Replace at least the top 5 daily "Take: [headline] — overblown or fair?" posts per week with real, submitted user questions** (you already did this once — "What's a small India habit that should be a national flex?" — do more of that, less news-aggregator reformatting). Gen Z's #1 documented trust-killer is unlabeled aggregator/AI-flavored content; this is the single highest-leverage fix before GTM.
3. **Have 3–5 short clips ready before day one of any campaign** — 15–30 second vertical clips of an actual live room moment, an actual "Fund it or Pass" verdict, or a screen-recorded scroll through real replies on a good debate. If none exist yet because rooms are still empty, that's gate #1 not being met — don't skip ahead.

## 4. Positioning for Gen Z specifically

**Not:** "India's public square" (too civic-institutional, sounds like a NGO or news outlet)
**For Gen Z GTM, lead with:** "The group chat where India actually argues" or "Pick a side. Prove it live." — keep the sided-debate mechanic front and center, since that's your genuine structural differentiator, and drop the "public square" framing in Gen Z-facing content specifically (keep it on the main site/brand, but don't lead with it in Reels/Shorts captions — it undersells the actual experience).

**The line to resolve the video tension explicitly, for use in bios/captions:** "No Reels feed. Just say it live." — this reframes "no Reels" from a defensive/nostalgic stance into an active choice for people already fatigued by algorithmic video feeds, which is a real (if smaller) documented Gen Z sub-segment, without pretending the mainstream preference doesn't exist.

## 5. Channel strategy

| Channel | Role | Why (grounded in data + product reality) |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram Reels** | Primary acquisition | Dominant Gen Z India format (Meltwater: comedy/meme/viral video content is 44%+ of Gen Z India consumption). Use clips of real debates, not produced ads. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Secondary acquisition, longer shelf life | Same clip content, repurposed; Shorts has better search/discovery longevity than Reels for evergreen debate topics. |
| **Telegram** | Community + distribution | 58.1% Gen Z India reach per Meltwater — highest of any platform cited. Launch a BaratX Telegram channel that posts "today's question" daily and links straight into the live room — lower-friction than a WhatsApp channel for a link-out flow. |
| **WhatsApp community** (already live) | Retention, daily nudge | You already have this — use it as the daily "today's question dropped" push, not a broadcast-only channel; that's the retention loop Gen Z's habit (heavy WhatsApp/Telegram group use) already supports. |
| **X / @getbaratx** (already live) | Credibility + hustle/builder audience | Keep this for Wedge B (builders/hustle) — X still over-indexes on people who'll actually debate startups and policy in text, which is a real but smaller slice of Gen Z. |
| **Campus ambassadors** | Seeding + Wedge A distribution | See Section 6 — this is your highest-leverage, lowest-cost channel given zero marketing budget signals so far. |
| **Meme pages / micro-influencers** | Amplification, not brand deals | Research is explicit that Gen Z trusts micro-influencers with niche followings over celebrities — target 5k–50k follower campus/culture meme accounts for paid or barter shoutouts, not large influencers. |

## 6. The Campus Ambassador program (redesign of Founding ₹150)

Your existing Founding ₹150 incentive is built for "whoever opens a live debate first" — for Gen Z GTM specifically, narrow and gamify it:

- **Recruit 15–25 campus reps** across 3–5 colleges (mix Tier-1 like Delhi/Mumbai/Bangalore and at least 2 Tier-2 cities, matching your own "Tier-2/Bharat builder" instinct from the product's existing taxonomy).
- Each rep's job: post one real daily-question take themselves, open or co-host one live room a week on a campus-relevant topic (hostel life, placements, fest season, cricket, campus politics), and share it to their own Instagram/WhatsApp story.
- **Pay structure:** keep the existing ₹150 Founding-room mechanic as the base, but add a small recurring incentive (₹300–₹500/month) for reps who hit a minimum weekly activity bar (1 room hosted + 5 real replies generated) — this is cheap relative to paid ads and produces the one thing GTM can't buy: real, non-bot content.
- **What they get beyond money:** "Founding Campus Voice" badge (extends your existing Blue/Gold badge system — explain it the same way your `/guidelines` page already does), and first access to any future monetized features.

This is deliberately not an influencer-marketing budget line — it's a distributed content-and-seeding force, which is what actually solves gate #1 and #2 from Section 3.

---

## 7. Content engine — what gets posted, and how often

- **Daily:** one 15–30 second vertical clip pulled from the best real exchange in the last 24 hours (a sharp reply, a funny "Fund it or Pass" verdict, a heated Agree/Disagree split) — posted to Reels + Shorts + Telegram. This requires gate #1 (real content existing) to be true first.
- **Daily:** the "today's question" itself, reformatted as a Reels-style text-over-video or carousel — not just a WhatsApp/Telegram forward, an actual native-feeling post asking the question directly to the platform's own audience.
- **Weekly:** one "room recap" — a 60-second highlight reel of the week's best live debate, captioned like campus gossip, not like a news brief.
- **Weekly:** one campus-ambassador spotlight — humanizes the Founding Voice program and gives reps a reason to keep showing up.
- **Tone rule:** captions and on-screen text should sound like a sharp group chat friend, not a startup's marketing account — this is the single fastest way to fail the "forced authenticity" test Gen Z research flags repeatedly.

## 8. 90-day phased launch

**Days 1–15 — Close the product gate (Section 3), zero paid spend:**
- Personally seed 10–15 real replies across 3–5 top debates in Entertainment/Sports
- Recruit first 8–10 campus ambassadors
- Publish 5 real clips from genuine activity (not staged)
- Target: at least 1 debate per Arena with 5+ real replies; 3+ real clips ready

**Days 16–45 — Soft launch, organic only:**
- Campus ambassadors go live weekly in their own networks
- Daily Reels/Shorts/Telegram posting begins in earnest
- Launch the Telegram channel
- Target: 25+ campus ambassadors active, 500+ real (non-official-account) posts across Arenas, 3+ genuinely viral clips (10k+ views each) — track which topics travel and double down

**Days 46–75 — Paid amplification on what's already working:**
- Boost the top 3 organically-proven clips with a small paid spend (this is far more efficient than paid-first, since you're now boosting proven winners, not guessing)
- Begin 5–10 small meme-page/micro-influencer barter or paid shoutouts in target cities
- Target: DAU among under-25 users trending up week over week; D7 retention ≥25% for new signups from campus channels

**Days 76–90 — Evaluate and decide:**
- Target metrics to hit before scaling GTM spend further:
  - **≥40%** of new signups in this window come from the under-25 Gen Z ICP (self-reported or inferable from campus-channel attribution)
  - **≥3** organically viral clips (10k+ views) directly attributable to real product content, not produced ads
  - **≥30%** D7 retention among Gen Z-sourced signups
  - **≥50** campus-ambassador-driven live rooms hosted with 5+ real participants each
- **Decision rule:** if these hit, scale the campus ambassador program to 10+ more colleges and increase paid spend on proven clip formats. If clips perform but retention doesn't, the acquisition layer is working and the product's core loop (Section 3) still isn't — go back to fixing real engagement before spending more on reach.

---

## 9. Budget guardrails (bootstrap-appropriate)

Given the product's current stage (near-zero users, no stated marketing budget), this plan is designed to be front-loaded with sweat equity, not ad spend:

- **Days 1–45:** ₹0–₹50,000 total — campus ambassador stipends only, no paid media.
- **Days 46–90:** ₹1–3 lakh — small boosted-post spend on proven clips plus 5–10 micro-influencer barter/paid slots. Do not spend more than this until Section 8's Day 90 metrics validate the funnel — paid spend into an unproven funnel is the single fastest way to burn cash without learning anything.

---

## 10. Risks — named honestly

1. **"No Reels required" as a product stance may cap ceiling growth**, since it deliberately opts out of the format Gen Z spends the most time in. The GTM plan works around this by using video for acquisition only, but if paid clip performance stalls even after organic proof, that's a signal the text/live-only product experience itself — not the marketing — is the ceiling, and worth revisiting per Section 8's decision rule.
2. **Campus ambassador programs can produce fake activity** (reps posting to hit quotas rather than real engagement) if not monitored — spot-check for genuine reply threads, not just post counts, weekly.
3. **Wedge A (Campus & Culture) and Wedge B (Builders & Hustle) pull in different content tones** — Entertainment/Sports content should stay light and meme-native; Startups content should stay credible and specific. Don't let one wedge's tone bleed into the other's Arena, or you risk the "sameness/lack of originality" turnoff the Gen Z research flags directly.
4. **This is still gated by the core, unresolved product question from the last audit**: whether Arenas actually produce organic conversation once real people show up. GTM cannot manufacture that — it can only amplify it once it's real.
